# Writing outreach messages: research synthesis

Four web research passes on 2026-11-16: cold first-touch email, follow-ups and channels, value framing and persuasion, and UAE/GCC norms. Almost all reply-rate data is vendor platform data (Gong, Lavender, Belkins, Hunter, Instantly), observational and not comparable across vendors. Treat numbers as direction, not targets. Gong's CTA and phrase data dates from 2020-21.

## Follow-ups

- Steps 2-3 carry most meetings ([Belkins 2025](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics): 58.6% of replies come after step 1), so the best material goes there, not only into the opener.
- Each follow-up opens with something new: an insight, a comparable case, a resource, an answer to a likely objection, a company event, or a smaller ask. Never "just following up", "bumping this", "did you see my email", bare "Thoughts?" ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/7-tips-for-writing-the-perfect-follow-up-sales-email-according-to-science): "Thoughts?" -20% meetings, "never heard back" -14%).
- Length: 50-120 words when it adds value, under 50 for a short nudge that restates why you wrote. Never repeat the pitch.
- Threads: follow-ups 1-2 reply in the same thread (a real "Re:"); a new stakeholder, angle or a gap over ~10 days starts a new thread with a new subject. Never a fake "Re:".
- Referral ask at the second-to-last touch, naming a person and why: "Would <name> be the better person for <topic>?"
- Breakup: how many times and why you wrote, timing assumed to be off, this is the last message, one easy question. No guilt, no "should I close your file". Its effectiveness has no solid data.
- Across channels: lead with the new angle and mention the earlier touch in one clause ("I sent the details by email on Tuesday").
- Cap about 4-5 emails; stop everything on any reply.

## UAE and GCC

- Relationship-based and indirect; saving face matters. A short, specific message with one courteous line, no pressure and no "final notice" ([trade.gov 2025](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/united-arab-emirates-selling-factors-and-techniques), [UK DBT](https://www.business.gov.uk/advice/prepare-to-do-business-in-a-foreign-country/understand-local-business-culture)).
- "Dear" plus a verified title by default. Sheikh/Sheikha, Dr. and H.E. only when the source shows them; "Al Shaikh" is a surname. Mr. with first or with last name are both in use; mirror the recipient after a reply.
- No Arabic religious greeting in an English cold letter. English by default; bilingual material helps with Arabic-speaking decision makers.
- No prices in a first message. Never touch rulers, religion, politics, alcohol, family, or criticise the prospect's current vendor.
- Every message carries a plain opt-out line. The PDPL (Federal Decree-Law 45/2021) is consent-first, with a right to stop direct marketing (Art. 17); ADGM makes objection to marketing absolute and DIFC gives a right to object.
- Regulated readers: banks and fintechs care about UAE data residency and CBUAE outsourcing approval (Circular 14/2021); VASPs about VARA outsourcing due diligence. Claim only what the sender can show.
- Timing: UAE Mon-Fri (Friday half day); KSA, Qatar, Kuwait and Bahrain Sun-Thu. Pause over Eid and National Day (2-3 Dec), go lighter in Ramadan (expected around 8 Feb to 9 Mar 2027).
- Not legal advice: the primary legal texts were unreachable and the findings rest on law-firm summaries.

## Conflicts left open

- Body length: 25-50 (Lavender 2023), 50-100 (Gong 2026), up to 150 (Gong 2021). Our email budget should sit near 50-90.
- Subject as a question: Belkins found question subjects open best; Gong says avoid them. Default to a plain topic statement.
- Proof with numbers: Gong penalises ROI figures; Lavender recommends one named customer with a metric. Reconciled as: a past result at a comparable client is proof, a projected return for the prospect is not.
