# Approach strategy and letter craft: second research pass

Five web research passes on 2026-11-17 build on [2026-11-16-outreach-messages.md](2026-11-16-outreach-messages.md):

1. sequence and cadence;
1. letter craft and measurable text features;
1. AI-written outreach and how to evaluate it;
1. what buyers say makes them answer;
1. deliverability and the legal minimum for one-to-one Gmail sending.

The same caveat holds as before. Almost every reply-rate number is vendor platform data: correlational, US-heavy and SaaS-heavy. Absolute reply rates differ about tenfold between vendors (Belkins 0.45%, Hunter 4.5%) because each defines a reply differently. No source isolates firms of 15-250 people, professional services, or the GCC. Treat numbers as direction.

### Replies

FirstSales says about 45% of replies are auto-replies (single vendor, unverified). Platform practice per reply class:

| Reply                           | Next move                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Out of office with a date       | Pause; resume 2 business days after the return with the pending touch unchanged.                                                                                                |
| Out of office, no date          | Pause 10 business days, then ask the user.                                                                                                                                      |
| Out of office names a colleague | Propose (never auto-add) one touch to that colleague that cites the auto-reply. Sources disagree on whether that's fair, so a human decides. It counts toward the 2-person cap. |
| Left the company                | Stop that person; offer a re-plan with the successor.                                                                                                                           |
| Positive                        | The human answers the same day with two time slots.                                                                                                                             |
| Not now                         | Stop; recycle on the date they gave, else after 90 days.                                                                                                                        |
| Referral                        | One touch to the named person, naming who referred them.                                                                                                                        |
| Objection                       | One answer, then stop.                                                                                                                                                          |
| Unsubscribe or hard no          | Suppress the company, and the person across projects.                                                                                                                           |

Sources: [LeadHaste](https://leadhaste.com/prompts/mine-out-of-office-replies-for-intel), [FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/out-of-office-replies-data/), [La Growth Machine](https://help.lagrowthmachine.com/en/articles/8045111-how-out-of-office-auto-reply-detection-works).

## Who reads it

- **Buyers check the sender.** 82% look at the provider's LinkedIn before answering (RAIN). A custom domain gets 5.2% replies against 2.5% for freemail (Hunter). 69% find inconsistencies between a vendor's website and what its sellers say ([Gartner 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)). The signature, the LinkedIn profile and the website have to tell the same story as the letter.
- **Buyers prefer to go without reps, but want an outside view on fit.** 61% prefer a rep-free experience (2025), rising to 67% (2026). The first contact works best as an expert's view on fit, not a pitch.
- **Out-of-market readers** ignore features and benefits. They value material that uncovers a problem they hadn't recognised (91% in Edelman-LinkedIn 2025 hidden buyers, secondary).
- **Professional services buyers** weigh relevant expertise more than before (+33%) and ask for referrals less ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/new-research-study-shows-significant-changes-in-professional-services-buyer-behavior), old data). "We work with law firms" beats "we do IT, automation and AI".
- **Law-firm triggers.** Client security requirements reached 27% of US firms, and 50% of firms with 100+ lawyers ([ABA 2023](https://www.americanbar.org/content/aba-cms-dotorg/en/groups/law_practice/resources/tech-report/2023/2023-cybersecurity-techreport)). Breaches hit about 29%. Name confidentiality handling up front.
- **Buying groups at this size are 2-5 people:** a partner or owner, the COO or operations head, and the IT lead or an outside IT provider. TrustRadius: 96% of tech buying groups have 5 or fewer; Forrester's 13 is enterprise-skewed.
- **Gatekeepers:** no data. The consensus is a three-line message an assistant can forward: who, for which role, why, "could you pass this to whoever handles X". Never dress it up as a client enquiry.
- **GCC:** mutual connections and referrals count (LinkedIn 2019: 41%), and WhatsApp is the default channel for consumers. WhatsApp only after the person engages; an in-person coffee is a credible later ask. The evidence is weak.

## AI-written letters

- **Detection is near chance, but tells are penalised.** In a 9-email test most people spotted fewer than 4 AI emails (Hunter 2025). People's heuristics for spotting AI text are flawed (Jakesch et al., PNAS 2023). What gets punished is the look: a predictable layout, sentences of equal length, compound adjectives, generic flattery. Hunter's own surveys conflict on how much buyers mind (47% / 67% / 69%).

- **Exposure costs more than authorship.** In 13 preregistered experiments, disclosed AI use lowered trust, and exposure by others lowered it more ([Schilke & Reimann 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v188y2025ics0749597825000172.html)). Never put a fact in a letter the sender couldn't explain on a call.

- **Sameness is the structural risk.** LLM output homogenises within one model and across models ([Jiang et al. 2025, "Artificial Hivemind"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954); Padmakumar & He, ICLR 2024). Letters to similar firms will converge on the same opener and ask unless something checks for it.

- **Evaluation without volume.** At 50-300 letters a month and 3-6% replies, an A/B test can't detect anything. Use three things instead:

  - pairwise judging against a fixed rubric, run in both orders, length-controlled, with a judge prompt other than the writer's (Zheng 2023, Panickssery 2024, Dubois 2024);
  - reviewer edits as the main signal: the diff between the draft and what was sent;
  - reply classes logged per angle, CTA type and touch.

  Self-critique without external criteria is unreliable (Huang et al. 2023), so a revise step must consume lint results and the rubric. Manually edited emails get 5.2% replies vs 4.4% for fully automated ones (Hunter).

- **EU AI Act Art. 50** likely needs no disclosure for one-to-one B2B email after substantive human review ([FLI guide, May 2026](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)). Keep a record that a human approved each letter. Not legal advice.

## Deliverability and the legal minimum

- **The bulk rules don't apply at 5-40 a day.** The bulk-sender rules (DMARC alignment, one-click unsubscribe) start at 5,000 a day. Every sender needs SPF or DKIM, a spam-complaint rate under 0.3% (target 0.1%) and valid DNS and TLS ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126), [FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414), [Yahoo](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)). At 20 a day one complaint is a large share, so extra touches carry real cost. Google's tooling won't show data at this volume.

- **Workspace limits:** 2,000 messages and 2,000 external recipients a day; trial accounts get 500. Exceeding a limit blocks sending for up to 24 hours, and spam senders may be restricted permanently ([limits](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/gmail-sending-limits-in-google-workspace)). Google's only ramp advice is to avoid sudden spikes.

- **Spam word lists are a myth;** no mailbox provider publishes one. Plausible but unquantified: link shorteners, shared tracking domains, open pixels, image signatures, attachments in touch 1, near-identical bodies. Hunter: open tracking off 7.4% replies vs 4.4% with it on.

- **Bounces:** no official threshold; vendors say under 2%. At 20 a day one bounce is 5%. Probing an address is unreliable on catch-all, Google-hosted and Microsoft-hosted domains, so guessed addresses carry most of the risk.

- **Law that shapes the text:**

  - **US CAN-SPAM** has no B2B exception: identify the message as a solicitation, give a postal address and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).
  - **UK PECR reg. 23** covers corporate subscribers too: no hidden sender, and a valid address for opt-outs ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/23)).
  - **GDPR:** legitimate interest is a possible basis, objection to marketing is absolute (Art. 21), and Art. 14(3)(b) asks for privacy information at the first communication.
  - **Germany (§7 UWG)** needs prior consent even for B2B email ([IHK](https://www.ihk.de/karlsruhe/fachthemen/recht/wettbewerbsrecht/werbung-was-ist-erlaubt-2459622)).
  - **UAE PDPL** Art. 17 gives a right to object; its executive regulations are reportedly still unpublished.

  Not legal advice; the ICO and UAE primary texts were unreachable.

## Conflicts left open

- **Emails per person:** 3 (Hunter) vs 4-7 (Instantly). Follow-ups bring 58.6% of replies (Belkins) vs 58% from email 1 (Instantly). The metrics differ.
- **Facts per letter:** Hunter's two details vs microtargeting adding nothing (Hackenburg). Reconciled as one hook plus at most one supporting fact.
- **Opener length floor:** 25-50 (Lavender 2023) vs 50-100 (Gong 2026, executives). Keep 50-100 for owners and partners.
- **Specific business case vs no ROI:** RAIN's executives want a business case, while Gong penalises ROI talk. Reconciled as before: an evidenced result at a comparable client is proof, a projected return is a promise.
- **Opt-out placement:** the law (CAN-SPAM, PECR) points to an opt-out path in every message. The sequence research would add one from touch 3. Our letters add it only for the GCC.
- **"Congrats on..." openers:** common practice, but fake compliments are a top dealbreaker (27%). Keep them only when the next clause turns the event into the problem.
